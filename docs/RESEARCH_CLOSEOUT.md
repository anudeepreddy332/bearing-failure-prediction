# Research closeout

The IMS study ended at Phase N and [D-045](decisions/DECISIONS.md#d-045--kill-the-phase-m-signal-policy-as-not-robust-beyond-the-clock). The original remaining useful life (RUL) prototype failed to transfer between the two documented damaged bearings. The target was subsequently shown to be an exact countdown of the shared experiment clock. A separate causal condition monitor produced bearing-specific behavior, but its predeclared comparison failed the burden and onset stability gates. The engineering and negative findings are the deliverables. No model or policy advanced to external evaluation or serving.

## What was built

The first implementation turned short vibration recordings into time-domain and spectral features, summarized each sensor observation, and added temporal and same-bearing cross-axis features. The notebook/parquet flow and legacy PostgreSQL pipeline were separate paths. The database prototype trained Random Forest and LightGBM, tuned LightGBM with Optuna, and exposed the saved model through a Streamlit dashboard and FastAPI endpoint. The database recreation audit found no writer for the intermediate `windows` table, so an empty-database end-to-end build was never established. [Original prototype tree at d780c28](https://github.com/anudeepreddy332/bearing-failure-prediction/tree/d780c28), [historical runbook](runbooks/PIPELINE_RECREATION.md).

The correction introduced a database-free split harness and root-cause analysis, then moved to hash-pinned source and physical identities, explicit terminal-outcome adjudication, sensor-local features, and deterministic validation. Phase D used 19 complete windows of 2,048 samples with a 1,024-sample stride per sensor recording. Its registry contains 17 feature definitions with per-recording mean and population standard deviation. Seven dimensionless feature families fed the Phase E diagnostic and Phase M monitor. The raw signal amplitude unit and calibration are unverified. [Phase D config](../configs/features/ims_set1_sensor_local_base_v1.json), [Phase D canonical manifest](../data/manifests/ims_set1_sensor_local_base_features/v1/canonical_manifest.json).

### Historical claims and run reconciliation

The legacy labeler took the latest timestamp for each of bearings 3 and 4 as zero, broadcast its countdown across the two sensor views, and marked the other bearings censored in the old schema. The later outcome audit supersedes that censoring interpretation. Before the split, temporal engineering grouped by bearing and axis for trailing rolls, EMAs, deltas and slopes, normalized by full-series means and standard deviations, and attached same-timestamp cross-axis summaries to each view. Even trailing transforms can draw from eventual test rows when the split is a shuffle. Legacy feature ranking restricted its SQL query to training rows and used correlation, mutual information and redundancy filtering, but those rows already carried the precomputed features. Optuna used `split='test'` to score every trial. Final evaluation queried the same partition. The weighted MAE was the trial scoring criterion, with weights `1 + 1/(proxy_hours + 10)` (at most 1.1 for a nonnegative target). The LightGBM fit retained its ordinary regression objective without weighted training. These code paths establish the design, while complete run logs and the exact historical DB snapshot are missing. ## Decision and scope

The [completed phase map](PROJECT_ROADMAP.md) traces Phases A–N to the versioned artifacts. There is no onward phase in this project. Set 1 offers one shared run and two documented damaged trajectories. Set 2 did not establish a supervised event-time target. The recovered archive remains unresolved. The last experiment rejected the frozen monitor under its robustness criteria. External validation, new labels, tuning, pooling, policy design, deployment and maintenance or savings claims remain outside this closeout.

## Resume bullets

- Built a reproducible vibration evidence pipeline for 2,156 eight-channel IMS recordings at 20 kHz, validating 353 million scalar readings and publishing hash-pinned identities for 17,248 sensor observations across four physical bearings.
- Replaced row-level validation with physical-bearing holdouts and purged blocks, measured 226.46 h MAE against the run-end proxy on two damaged-bearing trajectories, and showed that the shared experiment clock reproduced that proxy with zero-second error.
